## 1. The question

Does ranking on twelve-month relative strength and holding for 20 sessions pick
stocks that beat random names bought on the same days, out of sample?

Not "does momentum work" — that is settled in the literature and irrelevant to
whether it survives this account's costs, universe, holding period and $10,000
of capital.

## 2. The configuration

```
rs_lookback   252      twelve months
top_pct       0.10     the top decile by volatility-adjusted relative strength
hold          20       sessions, then re-rank
stop_atr      3.0      ATR(14) below the fill
trend_filter  on
entry_delay   1        signal at the close, fill at the next open
risk_pct      0.005    0.5% of equity per trade
max_positions 10
```

Total open risk is 10 × 0.5% = **5%, unchanged from the approved profile**.
This is the same money spread over twice as many names, not more money at risk.

## 3. How it was chosen, stated plainly

Chosen by the plateau rule from the `h1_selection` surface in the 2026-09-20
battery, which swept `rs_lookback` ∈ {21, 63, 126, 252} × `top_pct` ∈ {0.05,
0.10, 0.20}. **Twelve cells were seen before this one was picked.** The rule
selected the plateau centre (252 / 0.10) over the peak (21 / 0.10, +0.179R),
because the peak sat at the 62nd percentile against random while the plateau
sat at the 97th.

The risk figure was not searched for. It was set to fix a sample-size problem:
at 1% risk the 5% open-risk cap binds at five concurrent positions, which left
171 effective observations against a floor of 200. Halving risk doubles the
positions the cap allows, and doubled the trade count from 305 to 602.

## 4. What makes this different from a lucky cell

The percentile against random is **monotonic in the ranking window**:

| rs_lookback | same-day percentile | expectancy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 252 | 95th–98th | +0.141R to +0.147R |
| 126 | 87th–91st | +0.104R to +0.132R |
| 63 | 81st–86th | −0.130R to −0.150R |
| 21 | 48th–62nd | +0.156R to +0.179R |

Three adjacent cells at 252 all sit above the 95th. A dose-response is much
harder to produce by chance than one good cell, and it is the shape Jegadeesh &
Titman (1993) report. It appeared only after the split fix of 2026-09-20,
because unadjusted splits had been inverting the ranking.

## 5. The registered configuration on development (in-sample)

> **Superseded 2026-09-22.** The table below was measured with `--universe`
> unset. On `sp500_pit` the same configuration reads 45.0th percentile,
> p = 0.5504, PF 1.14, 1 of 3 regimes — failing three of six. Read the table as
> a record of what an unrestricted universe produces, not as this

Every criterion passes. **Development proves nothing** (docs/03 §0.4); this is

| --- | --- | --- |
| trade_count | 240 effective (602 raw) | ≥ 200 effective |
| expectancy | +0.165R | > 0 |

| vs_random | same-day p = 0.0240 | ≤ 0.05 |

Return +59.2% against SPY's +81.2% over 2010–2015. **It underperforms buy and
hold on the development split**, which is expected of a rule holding roughly a
third of the time in a bull market, and is not one of the criteria.

## 6. What each outcome means, decided now

- **Passes validation.** One out-of-sample confirmation of a configuration
  fixed in advance. It does NOT authorize real capital: that needs the test
  split (Stage E) and then a forward test (Stage F). It would authorize
  starting the paper forward test with intent.
- **Fails on the p-value.** The 97th percentile on development was the search
  talking. This is the expected outcome on base rates and should be recorded
  as such, not explained away.
- **Fails on the trade floor.** 2016–2019 is four years against development's
  six, so a shortfall here is arithmetic, not evidence about the rule. Recorded
  as inconclusive rather than negative.
- **Fails on regimes.** 2016–2019 contains the 2018-Q4 correction. A long-only
  rule losing to random through that quarter is informative about drawdown
  behaviour and is a fail.

No outcome licenses re-running with a different configuration and reporting
that instead. Two validation slots would remain; spending one to chase a miss
is the behaviour this file exists to prevent.

## 7. Two things to settle before signing

1. **The risk change is the operator's.** 0.5% per trade is not the approved
   profile's 1%, even though total open risk is unchanged at 5%. It was
   measured, not adopted. Signing this file adopts it for this configuration.
2. **Holm's family must be closed first** (2026-09-19 decision 2). Signing a
   second registration after this one runs changes the bar this one was held
   to. Either commit to this being the only H1 validation entry, or write the
   others now. `screener prereg family` reports what the family currently is.

## 8. What this replaced

An earlier draft of this file registered **hold 10, stop 2.5 ATR, 63-day
lookback, top 10%**, chosen from the 2026-09-18 battery. Its own drafter
recommended against signing it, and the data that produced it is now known to
have been defective in two ways: splits were unadjusted, which inverted the
momentum ranking, and 25 symbols were the wrong company. Its 63-day lookback

It was never signed, so nothing was spent on it. The whole of that draft is

The registry allows one file per hypothesis and **fails the entire load** on a
duplicate rather than skipping it. That is why this is one file: a guard that
silently skips a malformed registration fails open.

# Superseded draft, 2026-09-18 — kept as a record, registers nothing

## 1. The question

Does ranking large caps by 63-day relative strength, holding the top 10% for
10 trading days, and exiting at a 2.5 × ATR(14) stop pick stocks that beat
**random stocks bought on the same days and held for the same number of bars**?

This is a question about selection. The same-day benchmark
(`date_matched_benchmark`) keeps each trade's calendar and swaps only the
symbol, so timing can't count as selection skill.

## 2. The configuration, and how it was chosen

The configuration is the plateau centre of the `h1_exits` surface in the
2026-09-18 development battery. It was selected by docs/03 §0.7, the project's
existing rule, and not by the drafter:

| option | value | source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| hold | 10 | h1_exits plateau centre |
| stop_atr | 2.5 | h1_exits plateau centre |
| rs_lookback | 63 | `backtest run` default, which h1_exits held fixed |
| top_pct | 0.10 | `backtest run` default, which h1_exits held fixed |
| trend_filter | on | default |
| use_stop | yes | default |
| entry_delay | 1 (next open) | the original rule, written out explicitly |
| equity, slippage | $10,000, 5 bps each side | defaults |

**Not registered, and why:**

- **The best-looking H1 cell**: `rs_lookback=252, top_pct=0.10`, at the 97th
  percentile on the same-day benchmark. It is the *peak* of the `h1_selection`
  surface, and §0.7 rejects peaks.
- **The `h1_selection` plateau centre** (`rs_lookback=126, top_pct=0.20`). Its
  profit factor is 1.099 on development, below the 1.20 bar. It fails before
  validation.
- **The no-stop variant.** Its 2.9 SE advantage was measured in the isolated
  $5M portfolio at a 20-bar limit. In the tradeable portfolio at a 10-day hold,
  removing the stop makes H1 *worse*: +0.005R and profit factor 1.095
  (`h1_no_stop`, hold=10). The exit finding doesn't carry over to this
  configuration.

The hold is written out explicitly, so this registration's hash doesn't depend
on `HOLD_DEFAULTS["h1"]`. That default moved from 5 to 20 on 2026-09-18, after
this configuration was chosen, and the registration is unaffected.

## 5. The test

**Split:** validation, 2016-01-01 to 2019-12-31, and nothing else. **One
configuration**, leaving two of H1's three validation slots unspent.

**Pass requires all of:**

1. Every criterion in `backtest/performance.py`, as the code applies it.
2. **Same-day percentile ≥ 95th.** This is in the `[decision]` block below.
   Once signed, `backtest run` applies it and counts it toward pass/fail.
   *The operator should keep it or delete it before signing, not after.*
   - Why add it: the existing 75th-percentile bar passes a rule with no
     selection skill one time in four. The 95th is the usual one-sided 5% level
     for a single pre-registered test, and it isn't adjusted for the other six
     hypotheses (that's open decision 5).
   - This configuration reached the 94.7th on development, so it would narrowly
     fail this bar even in-sample. That's intended: the bar is set by what
     counts as evidence, not by what the candidate achieved.

## 6. Two problems with the test as it stands

**The regime criterion requires every validation regime to be positive.**
docs/03 §0.6 says "positive in ≥ 3 of 5 buckets", but no split contains five.
Validation contains exactly three: bull 2017, **the 2018-Q4 correction**, and
bull 2019. So in practice the criterion asks a long-only rule to finish
positive through a quarter when the S&P fell about 14%. That gap between the
specification and the code is the operator's to resolve. The resolution would
also be chosen *after* seeing H1 fail regime robustness on development, which
this document records.

**The gates below aren't met.** Delisted coverage isn't fixed, and CLAUDE.md
freezes validation until it is.

## 7. Power, estimated without touching validation

Development ran 566 trades in 6 years, about 94 a year, so validation's 4 years
should produce about **377**. The design effect measured on this configuration
is 1.5 (monthly clusters) and 1.2 (quarterly), giving roughly **250–310
effective observations**. That clears the 200-trade floor whether it's read as
nominal or effective. Too few trades won't be what fails this test.

## 8. What each outcome means, decided now

| outcome | consequence |
| --- | --- |
| passes 1 and 2 | Goes to the test split with **this configuration, unchanged**. One run, once. |
| passes 1, fails 2 | Beat a weak bar but not a conventional one. Recorded as **not validated**. Does not go to test. |
| fails 1 | **Failed validation**, recorded. H1 is closed as a strategy candidate. The two unspent validation slots are **not** to be used on nearby configurations; that would be the search the budget exists to prevent. |

Every outcome is recorded in `research_runs` and CLAUDE.md, including a
failure.

## 9. Why write this down if the advice is not to sign it

Because the configuration is now fixed. Whatever the operator later decides
about regimes, coverage, or the hold period, H1 can't be re-tuned toward
validation without the change showing up as an edit to this file in the git
history. A pre-registration written after those decisions would be picked with
knowledge they add. This one records what was known on 2026-09-18, including
that it fails in-sample.
